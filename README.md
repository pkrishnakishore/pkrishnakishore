# Krishna Kishore Pandalaneni

**Research across engineering, public policy, data, and evidence synthesis**

I bring engineering, public policy, and data analysis together to study complex problems. My work spans industrial R&D, UN evaluation, development partnerships, and collaborative research on evidence infrastructure.

With engineering degrees and a Columbia SIPA MPA (2025), I connect technical systems with the people and institutions that shape them. My interests include technology and governance, the use of evidence in decisions, and the organization of expertise.

[Research portfolio](https://pkrishnakishore.github.io/) · [Research background](https://pkrishnakishore.github.io/projects.html) · [Contact](mailto:kp3076@columbia.edu)

## What I bring to research

- **Engineering and systems:** measurement, test-data interpretation, technical validation, and comparative regulatory analysis, grounded in industrial R&D.
- **Policy and institutions:** stakeholder analysis, implementation context, and research on governance and development.
- **Data and mixed methods:** survey analysis, interviews, qualitative coding, comparative document analysis, and evidence mapping. Computing coursework includes Python, SQL, BigQuery, and dashboards.
- **Evidence synthesis and collaboration:** structured extraction, data-quality coordination, report drafting, and research implementation across teams.

## Why connect disciplines?

My approach combines technical and institutional perspectives around a research question. The [National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11153/chapter/4) emphasizes integration across fields; [Uzzi and colleagues in *Science*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1240474) examine knowledge combinations and citation impact; [Wang and colleagues in *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06221-2) discuss the opportunities and data-quality challenges of AI in science. These readings inform my approach; the projects above show how I put it into practice.
